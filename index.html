Jane — AI Voice Agent for New User Onboarding

AI · Onboarding

I conceived, built, and launched "Jane": a 24/7 AI voice agent embedded into the new user welcome journey. The problem

~50–60% of new GetGo users failed to complete their first booking within 30 days of signing up. Data showed that most activations happen within D0–1 of registration, with steep drop-offs after that. Users with zero bookings showed significantly lower in-app activity — signalling confusion about the service rather than lack of intent. There was no dedicated, on-demand support channel to address their concerns at the critical early stage.

What I did

Designed and built Jane from scratch using Retell AI — writing the full agent prompt, persona, conversation flow, error-handling logic, and knowledge base covering 20+ FAQ topics (booking, insurance, car usage, pricing, and more). Integrated Jane across three channels (In-App Message, Content Card, EDM banner) within the D0–29 new user welcome journey. Ran a structured A/B experiment (Sep–Dec 2025) to test impact on activation rate. Iteratively improved Jane's knowledge base and routing logic based on call transcripts — including fixing hallucinations and building urgency-detection logic to route live issues to human CX.

The outcome

Jane resolved >80% of user queries without human involvement, at S$0.90/call versus S$5.87 for a live chat ticket — a 6.5× cost saving. The Jane AI Content Card outperformed generic content (17.56% vs 13.84% CTR). The experiment confirmed Jane introduced no friction to activation, and the decision was made to progressively scale to 100% of new sign-ups by December 2025. Jane is now GetGo's permanent, always-on virtual assistant for new users.

Key design decisions

Jane's persona was intentionally designed to be "friendly, patient, and empathetic" — not just an FAQ bot. Conversation flow was structured with one-question-at-a-time logic and explicit scripts for confusion, dissatisfaction, urgency, and abusive language. Guardrails prevented Jane from handling account-specific or emergency queries, routing these to live chat or the emergency hotline instead.

The problem

GoFood new user W+4 retention deteriorated sharply from ~13–15% in January 2020 to 8% by the week of 16 February 2020 — a trend that, if unchecked, would erode cohort LTV and increase the cost of new user acquisition significantly.

What I did

Collaborated with GoFood's demand growth analyst to build a retention prediction model using first-7-day behavioural features, scoring new users as high or low probability to retain within 15–30 days. Designed and ran 4 experiment iterations testing incentive (10×10k vouchers) vs. non-incentive (push notification) treatments across both segments. Used chi-square omnibus and pairwise tests to validate statistical significance.

The outcome

Low-probability users responded strongly to voucher treatment — 3.05 transaction frequency vs. 1.91 in control, with CPIT of $0.85 vs. $1.56 for high-prob (50% cheaper). High-probability users transacted organically without incentives, informing a leaner, segment-specific spend strategy. Overall W+4 retention recovered from 7% to 13% by June 2020.
